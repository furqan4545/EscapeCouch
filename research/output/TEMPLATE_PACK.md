# Templates with minimal new filming

This replaces the rejected food-production pack. These are production structures, not finished source-specific assignments.

## 1. Source hook → matching cut → your line

Use a short funny or surprising source setup, cut into your continuation of its action or joke, and end with one relevant line. A future app result can supply the payoff when it fits the same problem.

Your work: one simple action or sentence at your usual location. Reject a source that needs special props, difficult choreography or multiple people.

Before filming, the brief must contain the actual source URL, source timestamps, last source frame, your required first frame, exact sentence and cut instruction. Selecting a suitable source is research work.

Illustrative adaptation: a clip about indecision cuts into you looking at an exercise list. This needs a real suitable source before it becomes an assignment. An unrelated shocking opener followed by a generic sales pitch is not the same structure.

## 2. Source clip → one-point reaction

Show enough of the original to understand it, then deliver a specific observation or punchline. Reuse one split-screen or cut-to-face layout.

Your work: one short response. Use your real training experience. A factual claim about effectiveness needs checking; a personal observation should be presented as one.

Before filming, the brief must supply the source segment, why it merits a response, the exact line and the edit layout. The user should not have to scroll indefinitely hunting for something to react to.

Illustrative adaptation: point out the practical difficulty of a needlessly complicated workout setup in a busy gym. No recreation of the whole setup is required.

## 3. Existing personal footage → setup → punchline

Select a shot already in the user's gym footage library. Add one relatable premise and a timed text change or cut for the payoff.

Your work: existing footage; an occasional brief face pickup if useful. Do not stage a new two-person sketch or repeat an entire workout for the camera.

Illustrative adaptation: existing footage of waiting between sets, with a joke about planned rest versus getting distracted. Use footage that supports the joke. This is an original proposal, not a verified reconstruction of a shortlisted video.

## Hard workload filter

Accept only candidates built from existing material plus at most one simple new recording. Measure the actual time on the first attempt before promising a daily workload. Finding sources, fact-checking and editing remain real work even when handled by someone else.

Exclude food preparation, shopping, product staging, elaborate skits, new locations and repeated transformations. Reusing a concept while repeating substantial physical preparation does not meet the brief.

One face account is the primary channel. Four supporting accounts are available capacity, not four extra daily creative obligations. Select the narrow app problem after the format shows repeatable attention and relevant viewer interest.
