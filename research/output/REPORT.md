# Revised TikTok research direction

Updated 22 September 2026 after the user's workload correction.

**The food recommendation is withdrawn.** Reusing a camera angle does not make buying, preparing, arranging and measuring new food a low-work production process.

The requirement is existing footage plus one short recording of the user. Research should supply the actual source, hook, script and edit instructions. Five accounts remain the ceiling, with the face account first.

## Corrected research priorities

| Priority | Structure | What the user records | Work needed around it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Funny or surprising source clip → matching cut → your line | One short action or sentence | Find the source, select the cut, script the connection and assemble the edit |
| 2 | Existing clip → specific reaction or verdict | One short response | Select a worthwhile source, check factual claims and prepare the response/layout |
| 3 | Existing personal gym footage → relatable setup and punchline | Usually existing footage; optional face pickup | Select a shot, write the text and time the reveal |

These are candidates for deeper inspection, not newly proven winners. Reject any version that requires shopping, preparation of food, product staging, another performer, a new location, an elaborate skit or a fresh physical transformation each day.

## Existing evidence

**Matching-cut transitions:** earlier direct inspection found [Currys' karate-to-laptop post](https://www.tiktok.com/@currys/video/7445627128420289825) at a displayed 15.1M views. [MediaCat's July 2024 report](https://mediacat.uk/brands-are-being-weird-and-its-working/) documented brands adapting the same stretcher-fall structure. This supports studying the editing mechanism across niches. It does not prove any random viral opener will transfer. The newer broad searches contained many unrelated tutorials and did not establish a fresh supply of suitable hooks.

**Reactions:** [this dermatology duet](https://www.tiktok.com/@dermangelo/video/7348500031562992938) had 6.6M views, published March 2024. Recent examples included [6,610 views](https://www.tiktok.com/@dermangelo/video/7682911522284195102), published September 2026, and [5,600 views](https://www.tiktok.com/@dermangelo/video/7676112210552311070), published August 2026. A [@thebeardedbard reaction candidate](https://www.tiktok.com/@thebeardedbard/video/7659240499898748191), published July 2026, had 511.6K. These are metadata-level candidates, not completed clip breakdowns. Source selection and a worthwhile original response remain essential work.

**Gym humour:** [@ramiftn](https://www.tiktok.com/@ramiftn/video/7665089891419688214) had 2.3M views at 15 seconds; [@hundo.fit](https://www.tiktok.com/@hundo.fit/video/7668744495046905110) had 677.7K at 14 seconds. These support interest in short gym humour, but do not establish that those particular videos require little filming or can be made by one person. The existing-footage version is an adaptation to test. Multi-person skits fail the brief.

There is not yet enough inspected evidence to call any of these a repeatably viral, low-work winner for this user. Next validation must inspect the actual clips, recurring examples within each series, lower-performing posts and the full production workflow.

## What a useful handoff looks like

Each filming brief should include an actual source URL, exact segment, cut point, one-line script, matching-shot instruction and caption. State all preparation needed from the user. “React to something viral” leaves the selection work unresolved.

The [revised template pack](TEMPLATE_PACK.md) defines the structures and workload filter. Its examples are illustrative; source-specific editing instructions remain outstanding.

Do not choose the app niche yet. Possible topics include exercise selection, occupied equipment, remembering workouts or sticking to a routine. They are hypotheses to test through the strongest manageable format, not app recommendations.
